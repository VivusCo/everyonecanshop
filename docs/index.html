The law in 30 seconds

  • Since 28 June 2025, webshops that sell to consumers in the EU must be usable for people with disabilities.
  • Very small businesses are exempt: fewer than 10 employees and up to €2 million turnover.
  • Authorities can demand fixes and impose fines. In Germany, competitors can also send formal warning letters.

Source: Directive (EU) 2019/882

Severity: HighWCAG 1.1.1 · 4.1.2

Problem
The colour swatches are images without text. The buy button is an icon without a name.

Blind customers using a screen reader. They can't choose a colour, so they can't buy.
Fix
Give every image and button a short text name.

Questions

Does the law apply to my shop?

Most likely, if you sell to consumers in the EU and have 10 or more employees or more than €2 million turnover. This is not legal advice.

Will an accessibility widget solve it?

No. A widget changes how the page looks, not the code underneath. Unnamed buttons and unlabelled fields stay.

How do you test without access?

We use your public shop like a customer: browse, choose, add to cart, go to checkout. We stop before payment.

Is our data safe?

We only see what every visitor sees. No logins, no customer data, no real orders. Our own site uses no cookies.

Do you fix the issues?

Your developers or agency do, with our fix list. Afterwards we re-test.

What if you find nothing?

Then you get that in writing, free. On real shops this is rare.

Is the report a legal certificate?

No. It is a professional test against WCAG 2.1 AA, the standard behind the law. It shows what to fix first.
