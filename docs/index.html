What waiting costs you

  • Customers who leave quietly

    If a button cannot be reached with the keyboard or a field has no name, a customer using assistive technology cannot finish the order. Most do not complain. They buy elsewhere.

  • Letters from the regulator

    National authorities can check your shop, ask for fixes within a deadline and impose penalties. The Dutch ACM has already tested large shops and published the results.

  • Warning letters in Germany

    If you sell to German customers: missing accessibility under the BFSG can also be challenged under competition law, for example with a formal warning letter (Abmahnung).

The good news: most barriers are small code changes once you know where they are.

Finding 1

Add to cart button: keyboard focus is not visible

Severity
High
WCAG
2.4.7 Focus Visible (AA)
Where
Product page, main buy button

What we saw

We moved through the page with the Tab key. The button receives focus, but its look does not change at all.

Who is affected

Customers who use a keyboard instead of a mouse, for example because of a motor impairment or after a hand injury. They cannot see which element is active and cannot safely add the product to the cart.

How to fix

Add a clearly visible focus style for keyboard users. :focus-visible leaves the look for mouse users unchanged.

.add-to-cart:focus-visible {
  outline: 3px solid #13212c;
  outline-offset: 3px;
}

Finding 2

Colour options are images without a text alternative

Severity
High
WCAG
1.1.1 Non-text Content (A)
Where
Product page, colour selection

What we saw

The colour swatches are images without an alt attribute.

Screen reader announces
"image", "image", "image"
After the fix
"Moss green", "Ocean blue", "Sand"

Who is affected

Blind and low-vision customers using a screen reader. They hear only "image" three times and cannot choose a colour, so they cannot order.

How to fix

Give every option image a short alt text that names the colour.

<img src="moss.jpg" alt="Moss green">

Finding 3

Quantity field and its buttons have no name

Severity
Medium
WCAG
1.3.1 Info and Relationships (A), 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value (A)
Where
Product page, quantity selector

What we saw

The field has no label, and the minus and plus buttons only contain a symbol.

-1+
Screen reader announces
"button", "edit text, 1", "button"
After the fix
"Decrease quantity", "Quantity, 1", "Increase quantity"

Who is affected

Screen reader users. They hear an unnamed edit field and two unnamed buttons, and have to guess what they do.

How to fix

Connect a label to the field and give both buttons an accessible name.

<label for="qty">Quantity</label>
<button aria-label="Decrease quantity">-</button>
<input id="qty" type="number" value="1">
<button aria-label="Increase quantity">+</button>

What we check

We follow the path a real customer takes: find a product, choose options, add to cart, go to checkout.

  • Keyboard path to checkout

    Can every step be done without a mouse, and is it always visible where the focus is?

  • Screen reader names

    Do buttons, images, product options and fields announce what they are?

  • Contrast and zoom

    Is text readable for people with low vision, and does the page still work at 200% zoom?

  • Forms and errors

    Are fields labelled, and are errors explained in a way everyone can understand?

  • Mobile

    Does the purchase path work on a phone, including zoom and screen orientation?

  • Accessibility statement

    The EAA asks you to explain how your service meets the requirements. We draft it for you.

Questions

Does the EAA apply to my shop?

It applies to businesses that sell to consumers in the EU. Microenterprises providing services are exempt: fewer than 10 employees and an annual turnover or balance sheet total of up to €2 million. If you are above that, it most likely applies. This is not legal advice.

How do you test without access?

We visit your public shop like a customer: browse, choose a product, add it to the cart and go through checkout up to the payment step. We use the keyboard, a screen reader, zoom and automated checks. Nothing to install, no logins, no code access.

Is our data safe?

We only see what every visitor sees. We never place real orders: we stop before payment. We do not need customer data, passwords or admin access. Our own site uses no cookies and no tracking.

Will an accessibility widget solve it?

No. Widgets change how a page looks, but they do not fix the code underneath: unnamed buttons, keyboard traps and unlabelled fields stay. Regulators look at what customers can actually do.

What do you need from us?

Only your shop URL. If you want, tell us which pages matter most to you.

Do you fix the issues?

Usually your developers or agency fix them, using our report: every issue comes with the place, a screenshot and what to change. Afterwards we re-test.

What if you find nothing?

Then you get that in writing, with what we tested, at no cost. On real shops this is rare: in the ACM test, 61% of the largest Dutch shops blocked ordering with assistive technology.

Is the report a legal certificate?

No. It is a professional test against WCAG 2.1 AA, the technical standard behind EN 301 549 and the EAA. It shows where you stand and what to fix first.
